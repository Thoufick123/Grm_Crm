1. Purpose
The WhatsApp Business & Customer Communication module is designed to manage high-volume WhatsApp customer enquiries, conversations, follow-ups, orders, payments, delivery communication, returns, and staff activities.
The primary business problem is that customer enquiries and orders are currently handled directly through WhatsApp conversations. When the account is logged out, the phone changes, or many staff members handle the same number, important conversations, customer details, and pending orders can become difficult to track.
The new module separates:
WhatsApp Communication → Customer Query → Customer → Order → Payment → Inventory → Delivery
WhatsApp becomes the communication channel, while the GRM system becomes the central business record.
2. Business Problems
The system must address the following problems:
2.1 WhatsApp Account Logout
The business currently depends on one WhatsApp Business number. Repeated registration or device changes can cause account/session problems.
2.2 Large Number of Customer Queries
During busy periods, the business can receive hundreds or thousands of messages.
Staff cannot reliably remember:
Who asked what
Who was already replied to
Who is waiting for a response
Who requested product photos
Who said they would order later
Who needs follow-up
Who actually converted into an order
2.3 Multiple Staff
Three to five employees may need to handle the same WhatsApp customer communication.
Without assignment and ownership, multiple staff members may reply to the same customer.
2.4 Customer Data Loss
Important customer information can remain inside chat messages rather than the business database.
2.5 Order Tracking
A customer may first make an enquiry and later place an order. The system must connect the enquiry and conversation to the final order.
3. Main Objectives
The module should:
Manage large numbers of WhatsApp enquiries.
Prevent customer queries from being lost.
Allow multiple staff members to handle customer communication.
Assign conversations to specific staff.
Track unanswered and pending conversations.
Schedule follow-ups.
Maintain customer profiles.
Convert enquiries into orders.
Connect WhatsApp orders with inventory.
Maintain complete conversation history.
Track staff activity.
Provide reports and analytics.
Maintain audit logs.
Provide a foundation for WhatsApp Business Platform/API integration.
Reduce dependency on a single physical phone.
4. Overall Architecture
The module follows this business flow:
Customer↓WhatsApp Message↓WhatsApp Inbox↓Customer Query↓Conversation↓Staff Assignment↓Product Enquiry↓Follow-up↓Order Confirmation↓Sales Order↓Payment↓Stock Reservation↓Picking / Packing↓Dispatch↓Delivery↓Return / Exchange if required

37. WhatsApp Security
The system should support operational security controls around the WhatsApp account.
Recommended business practices:
Keep one primary WhatsApp Business device.
Use authorized linked devices for staff.
Enable WhatsApp two-step verification.
Never share verification codes.
Review linked devices regularly.
Remove unknown devices.
Maintain regular backups.
Restrict WhatsApp account settings to authorized administrators.
Maintain system audit logs.
Do not use unofficial WhatsApp applications.
The GRM system should not attempt to bypass WhatsApp's authentication or security mechanisms.

59. Success Criteria
The WhatsApp module will be considered successfully implemented when:
1,000+ customer enquiries can be tracked.
Staff can work on assigned conversations.
Unanswered queries can be identified.
Follow-ups cannot easily be forgotten.
Customer history is searchable.
A query can be converted into an order.
Orders connect to inventory.
Multiple staff can work without repeatedly registering the main WhatsApp number.
WhatsApp communication and GRM business data are separated.
Important actions are recorded in audit logs.
The system can later integrate with the WhatsApp Business Platform/API.
Losing access to a particular WhatsApp session does not mean losing the corresponding GRM customer/order records.
